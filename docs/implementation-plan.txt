2. PRODUCT PURPOSE
The inbox represents unfinished work or messages needing further attention. Lasting importance, urgency, category, and current need for inbox attention are separate properties.
Examples:
An important completed insurance or financial record can be labeled and archived.
A routine request that still needs a reply belongs in the inbox.
A newsletter, expired promotion, or obsolete notification may be archived.
An existing label does not prove that work is complete.
Archiving a completed request does not teach that its sender's future requests are unimportant.
The application's Gmail write operations are limited to archiving explicit messages by removing INBOX, adding existing user labels selected by the user or an enabled policy, and reversing applicable application-owned changes. Keeping or deferring a message normally requires no Gmail mutation. Preserve read/unread state, stars, importance markers, and unrelated labels. Do not expose deletion, trashing, sending, automatic unsubscribing, automatic filter creation, or attachment execution as application actions. Archival retains the email in Gmail; do not describe it as freeing Gmail storage.
3. TIME WINDOW AND AGE POLICY
Implement two independent configurable concepts and make their distinction clear in Settings and the job preview.
A. Examination window: which messages may be analyzed and changed.
Earliest bound defaults to unlimited: represent it as no lower bound and omit the lower-bound Gmail search condition. Offer an optional inclusive start date. If a concrete fallback is genuinely required somewhere, use January 1, 2000 and explicitly document the limitation; otherwise do not impose that date.
Latest bound defaults to a rolling cutoff 14 days before the job's anchor instant. Thus the most recent two weeks are excluded by default. Make the relative number of days configurable, including zero, and also offer an optional fixed latest calendar date.
Use Gmail internalDate, not the sender's Date header, download time, or history ID, for message age and eligibility.
Use timezone-aware datetimes and canonical UTC instants internally. Default the display/calendar-policy timezone to America/New_York and make it configurable. Include tzdata for Windows where needed.
Define eligibility as earliest_inclusive <= internalDate < latest_exclusive, with an unset earliest bound allowed. For rolling mode, latest_exclusive = anchor_utc minus the configured number of 24-hour days. For a fixed latest calendar date, include that whole local date by converting the following local midnight into the exclusive UTC endpoint. Convert an earliest calendar date from local midnight into its inclusive UTC endpoint.
Freeze the anchor and resolved bounds when a job is created. Resuming the job must preserve them. A new maintenance job receives a new anchor.
Display the resolved interval and timezone, not just relative setting text. Validate malformed dates and empty/inverted windows.
Gmail search dates have timezone/boundary behavior that must not determine correctness. Use epoch-second query bounds with a small deliberate overfetch margin where needed, and enforce the exact millisecond bounds locally against internalDate.
Every rolling maintenance cycle must discover messages newly crossing the 14-day boundary even if they have had no new Gmail history events. Use persisted boundary sweeps and periodic inbox reconciliation, with deduplication and resumable checkpoints.
Also schedule time-driven reevaluation when unchanged eligible messages cross an archival-age cutoff, reach a relevant deadline, or finish a deferral. Reuse valid stored assessments where possible; mailbox history alone cannot drive these transitions.
B. Age-based archival policy: how eligible messages are handled.
Enable a configurable archival-age policy with a default of two calendar months. This is independent of the latest examination cutoff of two weeks.
Resolve the age cutoff from the job anchor in the configured timezone using calendar-month subtraction with end-of-month clamping, then compare UTC instants. Do not silently substitute 60 days. Define old as internalDate strictly earlier than that cutoff.
Old messages with no outstanding attention requirement should have a lower threshold to be archived. Explicit protections and known unresolved work take precedence. Conflicting or insufficient evidence goes to review.
Important completed mail can be archived whether labeled or unlabeled; an existing reference label may be suggested for unlabeled records. Importance or absence of a user label alone does not force completed correspondence to remain in the inbox.
Important mail whose completion/action status is uncertain should be reviewed. A reference label alone does not establish completion. User-designated action/protective labels prevent automatic archival as configured.
Use fast deterministic paths for clearly routine/expired material, and enough analysis to identify unresolved work or uncertain important messages. Do not archive unassessed messages solely because no model result or protection was available.
Settings must also allow disabling the age policy and using category-specific age overrides. A category override can inherit the global policy, use its own calendar-month/day threshold, or disable age-based archival for that category. Resolve an applicable specific override before the global default; conflicting applicable overrides require review.
Strict scope requirements:
Default target scope is inbox messages within the examination window; exclude Spam, Trash, and drafts.
Messages outside the examination window are not classification or forward-cleanup mutation targets. Do not send their bodies to AI. Minimal identifiers, dates, and state needed to establish eligibility or detect a newer reply may be fetched. Explicit undo of recorded previous app changes is the narrow exception described in section 10; it is never a way to expand a cleanup job.
Optional Sent/archived context is read-only, separately configurable, subject to the examination/content-privacy limits, and never makes those messages action targets.
Review may group messages by conversation, but all actions expand to explicit eligible message IDs. Never use thread-wide archive/label mutations that could affect recent excluded replies or other non-target messages.
If a recent excluded reply makes older conversation conclusions stale, defer/review the eligible older items without touching or analyzing the excluded reply body.

Lasting importance/value.

Current need to remain in the inbox.

Pipeline:

2. Apply enabled explicit rules and approved narrow recurring-pattern rules.
3. Use cached assessments where their content/context/policy dependencies remain valid.
4. Use relevant feedback examples and AI for items needing interpretation.

For automated decisions, configured protections and explicit applicable user decisions outrank inferred behavior. Conflicting rules or ambiguous scope go to review. 14. IMPLEMENTATION ADDENDUM AND RESOLVED TECHNICAL CHOICES

The following binding technical decisions clarify and extend Sections 1–13:

14.1 External AI Module Top-Level Definitions, Exception Binding, and Call Contract (Section 8)
- The user's external AI module and the synthetic demo AI module expose the following top-level definitions:

```python
from dataclasses import dataclass

class AIAPIsError(Exception):
	"""Base exception for this module."""


class UnsupportedModelError(AIAPIsError):
	"""Raised when an unknown/unsupported model identifier is provided."""


class MissingAPIKeyError(AIAPIsError):
	"""Raised when an API key is not found or is empty."""


class APIResponseError(AIAPIsError):
	"""Raised when the provider response does not contain expected content."""


class RateLimitError(AIAPIsError):
	"""Raised when a provider indicates a rate limit (HTTP 429) condition."""


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class ModelResult:
	"""Structured result for a model call."""
	text: str
	model: str
	provider: str
	duration_s: float


def call_api(prompt: str, model: str = DEFAULT_MODEL) -> str:
	"""
	Call the routed provider and return only the response text.

	Raises:
		UnsupportedModelError, MissingAPIKeyError, RateLimitError, APIResponseError,
		and any underlying provider exceptions.
	"""
	return call_api_result(prompt=prompt, model=model).text


def call_api_result(prompt: str, model: str = DEFAULT_MODEL) -> ModelResult:
	"""
	Call the routed provider and return a structured result (text + metadata).

	Raises:
		UnsupportedModelError, MissingAPIKeyError, RateLimitError, APIResponseError,
		and any underlying provider exceptions.
	"""

Exception Binding & Handling:

Upon loading the external AI module mod, the adapter inspects getattr(mod, name, None) for AIAPIsError, UnsupportedModelError, MissingAPIKeyError, APIResponseError, and RateLimitError, binding each attribute where isinstance(obj, type) and issubclass(obj, BaseException).

If a minimalistic test double does not export all five classes, the adapter safely falls back to an empty tuple () for any missing exception class in except clauses so isinstance/except checks never fail with TypeError.

UnsupportedModelError and MissingAPIKeyError are treated as configuration failures that immediately pause the AI stage and surface a clear status in the GUI without automatic retries.

RateLimitError is treated as a transient rate-limit condition using bounded exponential backoff with jitter.

APIResponseError is treated as an empty/invalid model response eligible for bounded per-item retry and then routed to the error/review queue.

Any other AIAPIsError or unrecognized underlying provider Exception is caught via a general except Exception as exc block, preserving the exception class identity and sanitized error summary in the error queue so the user can inspect and retry it.

call_api vs. call_api_result Usage:

To satisfy Section 8's requirement that no extra module function beyond call_api is strictly required, the adapter defaults to calling mod.call_api(prompt) (when no explicit model is configured) or mod.call_api(prompt, model=configured_identifier) (when an explicit model is configured).

Include an optional setting (ai/use_call_api_result, defaulting to False for strict call_api contract compliance, or selectable in Settings when hasattr(mod, "call_api_result") is true) that calls mod.call_api_result(...) with the exact same model-argument omission semantics to record ModelResult.model, ModelResult.provider, and ModelResult.duration_s in local telemetry/prediction metadata.

The synthetic demo AI module must implement the full surface above (AIAPIsError hierarchy, ModelResult, DEFAULT_MODEL, call_api, and call_api_result).

14.2 Subprocess Isolation and SQLite Ownership (Sections 4 & 8)

Run the external AI module inside a dedicated multiprocessing child process (spawn start method on Windows) that holds NO SQLite connections. Communicate via serializable IPC messages over multiprocessing.Pipe or Queue.

When catching exceptions inside the AI worker process, serialize the matched exception category (UnsupportedModelError, MissingAPIKeyError, RateLimitError, APIResponseError, AIAPIsError, or UnknownProviderError), actual exception class name/module, and sanitized message across IPC so the main process handles recovery deterministically.

Keep all SQLite connections inside the main application process using WAL mode, busy_timeout = 5000, foreign_keys = ON, and strict per-thread connection ownership.

Prevent concurrent job execution across multiple application instances using both a QLockFile in the per-user app data directory and a job_leases SQLite table with PID and heartbeat timestamps.

14.3 Canonical AI JSON Response Schema v1 (Section 8)

14.4 Deterministic Age Policy vs. Score Threshold Resolution (Sections 3B & 6)

After evaluating eligibility, explicit protections, prior manual overrides, and enabled deterministic rules:

Route any item with outstanding_action != "none", uncertainty != "low", missing_context == true, truncated/unparsed body, or contradictory signals (e.g., outstanding_action == "none" paired with keep_in_inbox_score >= keep_threshold) to REVIEW.

For remaining items where outstanding_action == "none" and uncertainty == "low":

If age-based archival is active for the resolved category/global policy and internalDate < resolved_age_cutoff_utc, recommend ARCHIVE when keep_in_inbox_score < age_archive_max_score (default 65, configurable), else REVIEW (or KEEP if >= keep_threshold).

If age-based archival is disabled or internalDate >= resolved_age_cutoff_utc, recommend ARCHIVE when keep_in_inbox_score < archive_threshold (default 25), KEEP when keep_in_inbox_score >= keep_threshold (default 70), and REVIEW for scores in 25..69.

14.5 Incremental Sync, Boundary Sweep, and Thread Context Discovery (Sections 3A & 5)

Three-Step Maintenance Discovery:

1. Fetch users.history.list from the persisted startHistoryId to reconcile label changes, removals from INBOX, and newly arrived messages.

2. Perform an examination-boundary sweep between the previous job's latest_exclusive (T_prev) and the current job's latest_exclusive (T_curr) using epoch-second Gmail query bounds with a 24-hour safety margin (label:INBOX after:{T_prev_epoch - 86400} before:{T_curr_epoch + 86400}), filtering locally against exact millisecond internalDate.

3. Query local SQLite state for existing INBOX messages that have crossed resolved_age_cutoff_utc, passed defer_until_utc <= anchor_utc, or reached a recorded deadline.

Thread Context Without Leaking Excluded Bodies:

1. Use users.threads.get(userId="me", id=thread_id, format="metadata", metadataHeaders=["From", "To", "Cc", "Date", "Subject", "Message-ID", "In-Reply-To", "References"]) to inspect thread membership, internalDate, and labelIds (INBOX, SENT, DRAFT) across the conversation without downloading out-of-window message bodies.

14.6 Canonical Hashes and Calendar-Month DST Arithmetic (Sections 3, 4, 6, 8, & 10)

Hash Definitions:

content_hash: SHA-256 of canonical JSON containing message_id, internalDate, normalized From/To/Cc/Subject/List-ID, normalized body text, and is_truncated.

context_version: SHA-256 of canonical JSON listing thread messages as (message_id, internalDate, has_INBOX, has_SENT) plus the sent_context_enabled flag.

precondition_version: SHA-256 of message_id, sorted relevant labelIds, context_version, and active protection/policy revision ID.

Calendar-Month Subtraction:

Convert anchor_utc to the configured zoneinfo.ZoneInfo timezone, subtract N calendar months, and clamp day = min(local_dt.day, calendar.monthrange(target_year, target_month)[1]) while preserving (hour, minute, second, microsecond).

Resolve nonexistent spring-forward wall times by advancing to the post-transition instant, and resolve ambiguous fall-back wall times with fold=0 before converting back to UTC.

14.7 Gmail Batching and Windows Credential Protection (Sections 5 & 9)

Batching: Default HTTP multipart metadata/body retrieval batches to 25 messages (configurable 1–50) with per-part response handling. Group live execution mutations by identical (tuple(sorted(add_label_ids)), tuple(sorted(remove_label_ids))) into users.messages.batchModify calls of up to 100 messages (configurable up to 500) after preflight checks.

Token Security: Store a compact JSON credential payload (refresh_token, token_uri, client_id, client_secret, scopes) in Windows Credential Manager via keyring (staying well within the 2,560-byte win32cred limit), with automatic fallback to a per-user file encrypted via Windows DPAPI (CryptProtectData / CryptUnprotectData via ctypes.windll.crypt32).

14.8 PyQt6 50k-Row Virtualized Tables and Safe HTML Preview (Sections 11 & 12)

Back large tables (Review, Plan/Simulation, History/Undo) with SQL-driven QAbstractTableModel subclasses that push sorting (ORDER BY), filtering (WHERE), and grouping to indexed SQLite queries with an LRU row page cache rather than loading 50,000+ rows into an in-memory QSortFilterProxyModel.

Render email previews in a subclassed QTextBrowser with loadResource() overridden to block all external/remote URLs, setOpenExternalLinks(False) with explicit user handling on anchorClicked, and strict HTML allowlist sanitization prior to rendering.
